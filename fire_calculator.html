Assumptions and methodology

Taxes: Federal brackets (2025) + provincial brackets for the chosen province. Basic personal amounts applied per person. The age amount credit ($9,028 federal) applies at age 65+. Pension income splitting (50/50 RRIF income) is applied automatically once both partners are 65+. Capital gains are taxed at 50% inclusion using a proportional cost-basis method.

OAS clawback: 15% recovery tax on net income over $90,997 (2025); fully clawed back near $148,451. Modeled per person.

CPP/OAS adjustments: CPP reduced 0.6%/mo before 65 and increased 0.7%/mo after (max 70). OAS reduced/increased 0.6%/mo from 65 (max age 70 for deferral; cannot start before 65).

RRIF minimum withdrawals: RRSPs convert to RRIFs by end of age 71; minimum withdrawals begin at 72 using the official CRA factors.

Withdrawal strategies: Meltdown — when retired and pre-CPP, drain RRSPs first while filling the lower brackets, then non-reg, then TFSA. After CPP/OAS start, prioritize non-reg + small RRSP melt to fit beneath OAS clawback, TFSA last. Non-reg first — spend non-reg, then RRSP, then TFSA. Proportional — pull from all three in proportion to balance.

Limits: This is a planning tool, not personalized financial advice. It simplifies many real-world details (US dividends, attribution rules, spousal RRSPs, GIS, eligible dividend gross-up, deductions, RRSP overcontribution, capital gains inclusion changes, etc.).
